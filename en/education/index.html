Start with the audience

Education for people working in public and private organisations is among IBDCyber's stated objectives. A proposal in this area should begin with the work participants do and the decisions for which they are responsible. Content should help them recognise a situation, ask an appropriate question and understand when specialist support is needed.

For managers, the emphasis may be on responsibilities, priorities and the limits of a decision. For teams involved in public policy, it may be on concepts, sources and institutional implications. The depth of an activity should reflect what its participants need to understand and do.

Learning in context

Carefully discussed cases can connect a concept to an actual choice. An activity might examine how an organisation prepares its communication, allocates responsibilities or assesses evidence of risk. Its value lies in clear reasoning and the opportunity to use what has been learned.

The institutional presentation establishes education as a purpose. It does not, on its own, confirm available courses, instructors, teaching hours or certification. This website does not accept enrolments. Any future programme will need to state those conditions before receiving participants.

Describing an education need

When contacting the Institute, explain who would participate, what they already know and the difficulty they need to address. The number of people, their working context and the desired timing help assess whether an activity would be appropriate. Avoid unnecessary personal information in the initial message.

A programme requires an agreed team, resources, materials and learning objectives. For immediate reading, the knowledge section points to external digital security resources with identified authors. These resources remain the responsibility of their publishers and are not IBDCyber courses or certifications.
