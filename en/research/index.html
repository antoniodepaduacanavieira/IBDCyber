Knowledge for a specific decision

Developing studies and research forms part of IBDCyber's stated objectives. This purpose covers cybersecurity and data protection, with attention to public debate. The editorial approach proposed for this area is to make the research question, the origin of its information and its intended use visible to readers.

An international comparison, for example, needs to consider differences in institutional powers, scale and resources. The existence of a practice in another country does not establish that it would produce the same result in Brazil. Explaining these differences makes the comparison more useful to those considering a decision.

Reading evidence carefully

Market reports, administrative records and academic studies answer different questions. Observation periods, sample coverage and definitions influence their findings. These details should accompany any figures used to support a recommendation, particularly when numbers from different sources are compared.

The website's knowledge section begins with a selection of external resources. Authorship remains with each originating organisation. The selection helps readers find information; it is not a collection of IBDCyber research and does not imply that the Institute endorses every conclusion drawn by another organisation.

Introducing a research question

Researchers, public bodies and organisations can describe a problem, the available data and the decision a study could help inform. It is also useful to explain what has already been investigated and any restrictions affecting access to information.

A project depends on an agreed scope, team, research conditions and publication arrangements. No research commissioning service or publication schedule is confirmed in this version. Future work should identify its authors, review process, sources and date so that readers can assess the contribution for themselves.
